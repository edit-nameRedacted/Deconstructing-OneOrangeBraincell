r/OneOrangeBraincell · April–August 2022

Typical Orange Cats

There's a joke on the internet that all orange cats share a single brain cell, and only one cat gets to use it at a time.

r/OneOrangeBraincell is the Reddit community built on that joke. It opened in February 2022 to collect "orange cat behavior": photos and videos of orange cats doing something baffling or, once in a while, something clever. It grew fast, from about 53,000 members at the end of May 2022 to about 175,000 by mid-September.

The project

These are the posts from that first summer: the top 40 of each month from April to August 2022. That makes 182 posts once memorial posts, and posts whose photo or video no longer loads, are set aside. Every post was coded by hand: whether the cat has the braincell, how you can tell, what went wrong, whether the cat is treated as a person, and how dumb and how cute it is. In addition, a small vision model (Qwen3.5-9B; Qwen Team, 2026) was run to collect each featured cat's look: its build and where its white fur is.

How to read it

  • Each cat stands for a group of posts. Its body is the typical cat in that group: the usual amount of white fur, plus any markings or build that turn up there more than elsewhere.
  • The tail carries the group's numbers. Segments show how the group's posts did on Reddit: reaching the top of the rankings generally meant a post stayed up longer, and thus maintained a high ranking. Stripes in shades of orange and white show what stands out about the group on each of the other hand codes: the thicker the stripe, the more of the group it covers.
  • The eyes: wide, wonky eyes mark the dumbest group on screen; big sparkly eyes mark the cutest.

Click a cat to look at its tail, and a piece of the tail to see the posts behind it. The heading switches what the cats are grouped by.

The vision model: a capybara

This photo was drawn to our attention because it was flagged for removal for not containing a cat. Human review confirmed there was a cat, and investigation into how the model came to its erroneous conclusion yielded surprising results.

The model that records each cat's look (Qwen3.5-9B, run locally) reasons in writing before it answers. Of the posts it read, Bread is the only one where it found no cat, and it did not get there easily. It struggled over cat versus capybara for longer, at greater length and with more second-guessing than over almost any other post:

Its reasoning begins:

1. Analyze the Image:

  • Subject: There is a large, furry animal lying on a wooden table.
  • Appearance: It looks like a very round, brownish-orange blob. The fur is short and dense.
  • Identity Check: Wait, looking closely at the shape and texture... this is NOT a cat. This is a capybara. It's a rodent, specifically the largest living rodent. The prompt says "In other_animals, name any animals that are not cats as a short plain phrase". So I need to classify this as an "other animal".
  • BUT, looking at the image again... is it a very fluffy orange cat curled up? No, the shape is too smooth and rounded, characteristic of a capybara's body. The fur texture looks like coarse rodent hair, not cat fur. The ears are barely visible or hidden, which fits a capybara lying down. Wait, looking at the left side, there's a small ear-like structure. But the overall shape is distinctly capybara.

It goes on like this for nearly 300 lines. It re-reads the prompt, wonders whether the post is a trick, decides that “cats curl into tighter balls” and that this is “a long, smooth cylinder”, and even claims to recognise the photo as “a famous meme image of a capybara”. Its answer: no cats; other animals, one capybara.

The rerun: told it is a cat

So Bread got a rerun of its own, with one sentence added to the prompt: A human coder has confirmed there is a cat in this photo. Code it as a cat. Told that, the model saw an adult orange tabby of ideal weight, about kg, with no white fur, lying on its side on a wooden table. That rerun is the look this page uses.

The commenters: bread

The subreddit agreed with the title. 55 of the 135 saved comments are about loaves or bread; the top one, at 481 points, admires a perfect loaf, and nine replies say only “Bread 👍”. Another commenter retraced the model's route in reverse: first bread, then a guinea pig, and only then, from the subreddit's name, a cat.

Pointer

Body and coat: median share of white fur, plus markings over-represented in the group (at least 1.3× overall and on 30% of its cats; 3 cats minimum). Eyes: wide, wonky eyes for the highest average dumbness in the view; big sparkly eyes for the highest average cuteness among the others; other eyes are random. Tail segments each have their own shade, white to brown, base to tip; a segment's length is the percentile of the group's median among all posts. Stripes: one per hand code, each in its own shade of orange or white, showing the group's standout value for that code (the one most over-represented against all posts, held by at least 3 posts); a stripe is as thick as that value's share of the group. 182 top posts, Apr–Aug 2022, memorial posts excluded.

The upvote and comment segments are the same as before. The stripes now show what each model makes of these posts, in place of the hand codes.
